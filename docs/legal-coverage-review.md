## What the organizer actually requires

The supplied `Кейс_ForteBank.docx.pdf`, section 3 and FAQ 8, explicitly provide **no exhaustive or closed list of expected violations**. FAQ 9 allows previously unseen event types. Therefore neither the current catalog nor an expanded finite list establishes complete hidden-test coverage. The brief instead requires detecting observable possible violations, grounding each in an applicable open RK source, preserving evidence/timestamps and disclosing limitations. Section 7 expressly excludes findings that need unavailable bank systems or logs.

The brief does **not** require manually annotating videos before analysis. The user's revised instruction authorizes a fully automated **local first pass**. Facts and candidate intervals can be produced locally and evaluated by deterministic source/temporal gates. The mandatory language that results assist human checking describes the result's status; it does not create a mandatory manual processing step. Manual annotation is not an inference prerequisite.

The **100% detection goal** and **under-10-second goal** are user targets, not verified results. The organizer's published timeout is five minutes. Legal research cannot establish model accuracy, latency or complete recovery of hidden event types.

The supplied brief describes staged bank-cash footage, one camera, one cash counter, with cashier, clients and collectors (FAQ 3). That is usable trusted deployment scope for automated bank-cash analysis; no per-clip manual label is needed to establish the competition's domain. Actual visible cash-workstation and procedure facts must still be established. That scope does not prove that a room is a vault, someone is authorized, or a person belongs to a collection crew.

The first four proposals can use local automated facts and temporal state without new private bank-system access. The fifth needs unusually complete room context and a positively distinguished internal procedure. The sixth needs a new trustworthy room scope not established by the brief's one-counter setup. These are applicability conditions that an automatic first pass must respect; they are not requests for manual annotation.

## Reassessment of the five disabled gap categories

| Gap | What can be observed automatically | What is genuinely unavailable or unsupported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access by another person | Body crossing of a visible boundary; room entry; accompaniment | No. 231 paragraph 75 allows responsible persons and persons admitted by management authorization with accompaniment. Appearance does not reveal designation or authorization. No. 14 paragraph 8 requires an isolatable/closable service entrance by design; it does not prohibit every open-door moment. No. 14 paragraph 21 concerns collection loading areas and third parties, whose status is not established by clothing. |
| No visible identity document | Physical/digital document presentation or handling, without identifying the face | No. 231 paragraphs 19(1), 31(1), 7 and 10-1 allow digital-document workflows. No paper seen does not establish no verification. System verification and identity matching are unavailable; face identification is prohibited by the organizer. |
| Phone, clothing or gesture | The physical observation itself | No applicable general prohibition was verified in No. 231/No. 14. Collector equipment provisions (103 and 109-6) concern actual equipment availability and departure, not a blanket office clothing rule. No internal ForteBank rulebook was supplied; FAQ 32 says expected events do not rely on internal-only rules without an open norm. |
| Controller not seen | A narrow entire-room, exactly-one-person internal procedure can be checked, as proposed above | If another person exists, their designated controller role cannot be established from clothing, position or appearance. Customer receipt/issue has an explicit paragraph 42 exception. A desk crop cannot show that nobody else is present. |
| Vault storage/accounting/keys | Storage location and fixtures in an established vault may be observable, as proposed above | Paragraphs 74, 78–79 need system/accounting/inventory data. Paragraphs 75–76 need responsible-person designation. Paragraph 80 expressly delegates key/stamp/code handling to internal bank documents. An object handover or open safe alone cannot establish unauthorized key use or an accounting mismatch. |

Two plausible additions remain unsuitable for automatic legal findings on gesture alone. Paragraphs 24(3)(4)/107(1)(2) require integrity/seal **checking**, which may be visual without touching or turning a bag. Paragraphs 19(6)/31(6) require a confirmation document, whose permitted delivery channel may not be visible. “No touching” and “no paper handed over” must not be converted into their respective omissions. Visible damage, document handling and transfers can still be neutral observations for further local reasoning.

Similarly, observing one collector at the counter does not establish an undersized crew: the driver/other members may be outside, and paragraph 99 permits two-person crews in specified circumstances. A visible helmet not being worn is not equivalent to equipment being unavailable under paragraphs 103/109-6.
