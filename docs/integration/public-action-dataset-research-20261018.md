# Public action and contact data for Forte

Checked against publisher sources on 18 October 2026. This is a research and selection record; no dataset was downloaded or model trained by this review. Object-only sources are covered in [public-dataset-research-20261018.md](public-dataset-research-20261018.md).

The useful public data supports hand geometry, contact, object transfer, and generic temporal features. This search did not establish a commercially usable, annotated public dataset of safe-key abandonment, cash authenticity checks, cash recount omissions, or partial cash handovers. Generic action labels must not be converted into bank violation labels.

Human/robot pairs, all synchronized cameras, and repeated user/scene setups must remain grouped.

## A permitted modern tracking option

The current [Google DeepMind TAPNet release](https://github.com/google-deepmind/tapnet/blob/main/README.md) explicitly covers released TAPIR, BootsTAPIR, TAPNext, TAPNext++, and TRAJAN checkpoints under Apache-2.0. Its RGB-Stacking and RoboTAP videos/annotations are CC BY 4.0. This makes it a candidate for evaluating point tracking without relying on CoTracker3's non-commercial weights; it does not establish acceptable speed or key visibility on Forte footage.

The [official tracking-data guide](https://github.com/google-deepmind/tapnet/blob/main/tapnet/tapvid/README.md) links [RoboTAP](https://storage.googleapis.com/dm-tapnet/robotap/robotap.zip) and [RGB-Stacking](https://storage.googleapis.com/dm-tapnet/tapvid_rgb_stacking.zip). These contain video, point trajectories, and occlusion labels, not contact or bank violations. **Keep designated tracking benchmark sets for evaluation.** The guide identifies Kubric as its training source; any synthetic generator and embedded assets need their own license/provenance pins. Benchmark-data ingestion is not a shortcut to a training set.

## Leakage and proof requirements

1. Lock a source manifest before training: original URL/revision, archive and license hashes, permitted subset, original episode/participant/scene/camera IDs, label origin, and any derivatives. Save attribution with the artifact. Data, code, pretrained weights, object meshes, and pseudo-label teachers are separate assets.
2. Split source recordings before frame extraction or augmentation. Group synchronized views, participant sessions, captures of the same grasp, robot/human pairs, and near duplicates. Hold out whole cameras/scenes/participants where available. Re-encoded or cropped copies are the same source group.
3. Train public-data auxiliary tasks on their real labels. Preserve uncertain contact, occluded objects, and unobserved actions as unknown. A hand near a detector does not prove a note was authenticated; an unobserved count is not a verified omission.
4. Fine-tune bank event classifiers only on separate reviewed examples that establish actors, object identity, order, visibility, and the relevant rule. Public contact data cannot enable `NO_AUTHENTICITY_CHECK`, `NO_RECOUNT`, or partial-handover alarms by itself.
5. Keep the supplied Forte clips outside training and model-selection datasets. Because they have already informed engineering and candidate acceptance, describe their score as a development regression result. Claims about new videos need a fresh untouched target-domain hold-out with predeclared thresholds and no repeated tuning against it.
6. Use fixed held-out metrics for false alarms, misses, event time, and end-to-end latency; count failed jobs explicitly. A perfect finite test result is a target, not a guarantee of perfect performance on future footage.
